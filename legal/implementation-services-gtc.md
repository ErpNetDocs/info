## Principles of these Terms

These Terms set out a standard model for managed implementation of business software. The following principles describe the balance the Terms are designed to achieve. They guide the interpretation of the Articles but do not create obligations separate from them. If a principle and an Article appear to conflict, the Article prevails.

1. **Managed implementation towards the Client's objectives.** The Client engages the Contractor to organise and perform implementation work in the direction of the Principal Objectives indicated in the Order, under the Client's decisions. The Client does not hire individual workers, and does not buy a turnkey system or a guaranteed result; it pays for the expert work and Project Management actually provided.
2. **The Contractor organises, the Client decides.** The Contractor's project manager plans, proposes and coordinates the work, so that the Client does not need to know in advance every step an implementation requires. The Client is kept informed, sets business priorities, approves the proposed work expressly or by not objecting to it, and may stop future work at any time.
3. **Professional responsibility without an overall guarantee.** The Contractor is responsible for the professional quality of its work and corrects its own errors without additional charge within the agreed remediation period. Because the outcome also depends on the Client's decisions, data, people and changing requirements, the overall Go-Live date, budget and result are not guaranteed unless expressly agreed.
4. **Implementation is a joint effort.** The Client provides timely decisions, information, access and the participation of its people, and validates the solution for its business. Delays on either side are addressed openly and reflected in the plan.
5. **Pricing follows the nature of the work.** Exploratory and changing work is charged on a Time-and-Materials basis. Clearly defined results may be agreed as Fixed-Price Tasks with acceptance criteria. Continuing management is covered by a monthly Project Management Fee, and metered AI resources are charged only on agreed tariffs. The same work or resource is never charged twice.
6. **Transparency and cost control.** Time is recorded and reported monthly, the Client is warned before material estimates are exceeded, and any expressly agreed budget limit is respected. An estimate is not a cap, and a cap is never presumed.
7. **One Order, flexible work.** A single Order covers the whole project. Tasks, applications and work packages are added within its objectives without new contracts. Only a material change to the objectives requires the written agreement of both Parties.
8. **A defined lifespan.** Each Order has a defined term. Ordinary pauses do not end it, but it does not continue indefinitely. After the implementation, the Client moves to support or separately ordered services.
9. **Freedom to exit.** Either Party may end the Order on 30 days' notice. The Client pays for the work performed up to the end of the notice period and for commitments already made, and keeps its rights in the results delivered and paid for. Months already reported and accepted are not reopened, and the fees paid for them are not refunded.
10. **Fair and fast settlement.** Reporting, review and invoicing follow a short, predictable cycle. Undisputed amounts are paid on time, and once the monthly review period has passed (or the invoice has been paid), the Services reported for that month are accepted. Defects that could not have been found in an ordinary review can still be raised under Article 35.
11. **The Client's solution is the Client's to use and develop.** The Client receives broad, perpetual rights to use, modify and have others maintain its configurations and applications, and its data always remains its own. The Contractor keeps its general know-how, tools and reusable components.
12. **Clear allocation of roles.** Software subscriptions, hosting and platform services remain governed by their own terms. Each Party is responsible for what it actually controls, and only the Contractor named in the Order is responsible for its implementation Services.

## II. Definitions

**Art. 6. Implementation Engagement and Systems.**

1. An **"Implementation Engagement"** is an actively managed project under one Order, directed towards agreed Principal Objectives and Scope for the introduction, extension, integration or material reconfiguration of software-supported business processes and Systems for the Client, and involving both Professional Services and Project Management.
2. **"Systems"** means ERP.net, Operator.net and other ERP.net Corporation products and related applications, as relevant to the Order, together with any third-party software, services or environments expressly identified in the Order as part of the implementation. Including a System in the Order does not change its licence terms or impose infrastructure obligations on the Contractor beyond the agreed Services.
3. The Implementation Engagement is defined by its Principal Objectives and Scope, not by the number of applications, work packages, integrations or Tasks created in its course.

**Art. 7. Professional Services and charging models.**

1. **"Professional Services"** are implementation-related expert activities, including those performed in Tasks initiated by the Contractor's project manager or the Client. Unless expressly agreed as a Fixed-Price Task, they are charged for the time actually and reasonably spent at the agreed hourly or daily rates (the **"Time-and-Materials"** model).
2. A **"Fixed-Price Task"** is a defined Task accepted by both Parties under Article 27 for an agreed fixed price and specified deliverable or result. It is charged on the terms accepted for that Task rather than by reference to the Contractor's actual working hours.
3. Charges for AI or similar usage-based resources are governed by Article 44. The Project Management Fee is governed separately by Article 36.

**Art. 8. Project Management and Project Management Fee.**

1. **"Project Management"** is the continuing management and coordination of an Implementation Engagement as described in Article 24.
2. The **"Project Management Fee"** is the recurring monthly fee specified in the Order for those activities. It is distinct from charges for Professional Services and from any software subscription fee.

**Art. 9. Order.** An **"Order"** is the accepted document identifying, at a minimum, the Parties, **Principal Objectives and Scope**, the relevant Systems, the implementation project's start date and agreed term, the Project Management Fee, the applicable Professional Services rate schedule, the Client Representative, the Contractor's designated Coordinator or project manager, the agreed channel for operational records and any specifically agreed constraints or additional commitments. The Order may also identify budgets, authorisation limits and tariffs for usage-based resources, and any amount under Article 21(4).

**Art. 10. Task.** A **"Task"** is a specific work assignment that either (a) the Client Representative requests and the Contractor accepts, or (b) the Contractor's project manager initiates in reasonable pursuit of the Principal Objectives and Scope and records and communicates under Article 26, and which is deemed assigned by the Client in accordance with Article 26(2). A Task may be recorded, prioritised and tracked in the agreed project-management system, ticket, email or other durable written channel. Tasks need not be listed in the original Order, and routine project-template Tasks may be initiated without an individual Client request. Fixed-Price Tasks require the Client's specific agreement under Article 27.

**Art. 11. Personnel and roles.**

1. A **"Specialist"** is an employee or subcontractor engaged by the Contractor to perform Services.
2. A **"Level"** is a qualification or billing category of Specialist, such as junior, regular, senior, architect or project manager, as specified in the applicable rate schedule.
3. The **"Coordinator"** is the Contractor's designated contact responsible for organising the project workflow. During an active Implementation Engagement, the Coordinator will ordinarily be the project manager or a person authorised by that project manager.
4. The **"Client Representative"** is the Client's designated person authorised to request Tasks, set operational priorities, receive notification of Contractor-initiated Tasks, provide business decisions and communicate approvals within the limits specified in the Order. The Client shall keep the Contractor informed of any replacement. Approval of changes to the Order requires the authority specified in Articles 4 and 28.

**Art. 12. Objectives, hours, plans and capacity.**

1. **"Principal Objectives and Scope"** means the principal business or operational outcomes towards which the Services are directed, the business units, locations, processes and Systems initially included, and any material exclusions stated in the Order. They indicate the direction of the Engagement as provided in Article 20(2), may evolve as the Client's needs become clearer during the implementation, and are not a measure of the Contractor's performance or a result owed by the Contractor.
2. **"Estimated Hours"** are a non-binding forecast of likely time-based Professional Services effort. They are neither a fixed-price undertaking nor a maximum authorisation.
3. **"Maximum Authorised Hours"** or a **"Budget Cap"** exists only where expressly designated as such in the Order or a later written approval. A Budget Cap shall specify the categories of charges to which it applies; no estimate is presumed to be an all-inclusive project cap.

5. **"Reserved Capacity"** is a minimum amount of Specialist time per month which the Contractor commits to make available, and the Client commits to pay for, only where expressly identified as Reserved Capacity in the Order.
6. **"Project Plan"** means an operational plan indicating Tasks, dependencies, priorities, target dates or milestones, as updated during the implementation. It is a planning instrument within the meaning of Article 20(2).
7. **"Go-Live"** means commencement of productive business use of a relevant part of the implementation, as determined by the Client in coordination with the Contractor.

## III. The Implementation Engagement Order

**Art. 13. One Order per implementation project.**

1. An Order covers the implementation project described in it as a whole. Separate Tasks, applications (including AI-assisted applications), integrations, automations, work packages and phases undertaken for that project do not ordinarily require separate Orders, regardless of whether individual Tasks are priced on a time basis or at a fixed price.
2. A materially independent new project, or work outside the agreed Principal Objectives and Scope, may require a new Order or a written amendment. Neither Party may unilaterally expand the Principal Objectives and Scope.

**Art. 14. Principal Objectives, Scope and commercial parameters.**

1. Every Order shall describe the **Principal Objectives and Scope** sufficiently to direct and prioritise the implementation. It shall identify the relevant business entities, locations, processes and Systems as appropriate, while recognising that individual Tasks and detailed technical solutions may be developed during performance.
2. The Order shall specify the Professional Services rates or an identifiable rate schedule, the positive Project Management Fee, and any applicable Reserved Capacity, Budget Cap, spending authority, initial estimate, indicative milestones, target dates, dependencies, assumptions, agreed travel arrangements or special binding commitments. AI Usage Charges may be enabled by tariff and budget as provided in Article 44.
3. Whether Principal Objectives, target dates or milestones are binding is governed by Article 20(2). A specifically guaranteed deliverable or result must be expressly designated as binding in the Order, together with its acceptance criteria.
4. Project Plan adjustments and the addition, removal or reprioritisation of individual Tasks within the existing Principal Objectives and Scope do not require an amendment to the Order. Material changes to those Objectives or Scope require written approval by both Parties in accordance with Article 28.

**Art. 15. Defined term and maximum original duration.**

1. Each Order shall state a start date and an end date. Its initial term shall not exceed **24 months** from the start date. If an accepted Order inadvertently omits an end date, its initial term ends **12 months** after its start date, subject to earlier termination under these Terms.
2. Except as provided in paragraph 4, an Order does not renew automatically. The Parties may expressly extend it by a written amendment identifying a new fixed end date, including beyond the 24-month limit in paragraph 1. No extension is implied from continuing discussions, an unfinished project or unspent Estimated Hours.
3. Subject to paragraph 4 and Article 27(7), reaching the original or extended end date terminates the Order without a further notice, subject to settlement and the survival provisions of these Terms.
4. If, after the end date, the Contractor continues to perform Services at the Client's request and the Client pays without objection the Project Management Fee invoiced for the period after the end date, the Order is deemed extended from the original end date on a month-to-month basis. The invoice shall identify that fee as relating to the period after the end date. Such tacit extension: (a) ends at the latest 24 months after the start date of the Order; (b) may be ended by either Party under Article 47; and (c) does not change any other term of the Order. If the fee for that period is disputed or not paid when due, no tacit extension arises and Article 50(3) applies to Services performed after the end date.

**Art. 16. Estimates and expenditure controls.**

1. Estimated Hours, Estimated Days and estimated fees are planning figures and do not guarantee the amount of effort, price or completion date. Time-based Professional Services are charged for actual work, except for expressly agreed Scheduled Time cancellation charges or Reserved Capacity. Fixed-Price Tasks and AI Usage Charges follow their separate pricing rules.
2. The Contractor shall take reasonable steps to alert the Client when approximately **80%** of a material estimate has been used and shall provide an updated estimate when reasonably practicable. Failure to give this warning does not convert an estimate into a cap, but does not relieve the Contractor of its duty of professional care.
3. Unless the Order expressly states a Budget Cap or Maximum Authorised Hours, authorised implementation work may continue beyond the estimates at applicable rates, subject to the agreed Task procedure and the Client's right to instruct a pause or terminate the Order. In particular, the Contractor's project manager may continue reasonable in-scope Time-and-Materials Tasks under Article 26.

Reaching a limit suspends the unauthorised additional work; it does **not** automatically terminate the Order or end the Project Management Fee. AI Usage Charges and Fixed-Price Task fees count toward a Budget Cap only to the extent expressly covered by it.
5. The Parties shall promptly discuss necessary changes to the Project Plan or budget when a binding limit is reached or reasonably likely to be reached.

**Art. 17. Reserved Capacity.**

1. Reserved Capacity applies only when expressly ordered, including the relevant period, Levels, minimum monthly hours and rates. An agreed schedule, target date or forecast does not create Reserved Capacity.
2. The Contractor shall make the Reserved Capacity reasonably available during the relevant month, taking account of a mutually agreed working schedule and the Client's reasonable cooperation. 3. Actual work performed within the Reserved Capacity is credited towards the monthly minimum. Unused hours do not carry forward unless the Order expressly provides otherwise. Hours worked beyond the minimum are billed at the applicable rates.
