# Lead Management in @@name

## Why Lead Management Is There For You

Every new business relationship begins somewhere. 
It might be an inquiry from a potential customer, a conversation at an exhibition, a response to a marketing campaign, or a referral from an existing partner.

These initial interactions represent potential business opportunities. 
However, not every expression of interest is ready to become an actual sales deal. 
Before moving forward, businesses need to understand who the prospect is, what they are interested in, and whether there is a genuine opportunity to pursue. 

**Lead Management in @@name** helps your marketing and sales teams organize this process in an action-oriented manner, turning initial interest into structured, qualified prospects that can progress toward real business opportunities.

Within **@@name**'s Customer Relationship Management (CRM) system, a **Lead** represents a potential customer whose interest has yet to be developed into a sufficiently qualified sales opportunity.  

Lead Management provides a dedicated environment for gathering initial information, identifying people and companies, qualifying prospects, assigning responsibility, and preparing leads for further engagement through deals, quotation, and sales.  

Leads are preliminary records that describe the potential customer, their contact, description, and their interest you are trying to capture.  
And that is why, unlike sales opportunities, leads can contain incomplete, unstructured, or preliminary information. 
This allows teams to organize incoming interest without prematurely creating formal customer or sales records, keeping the environment for actual customers and deals clean and uncluttered.  

## Capture and Organize Potential Customers

When a prospect first approaches your business, the available information might be limited to their name, company, contact details, and some general interest in your offerings.  

**@@name** allows this information to be recorded in a lead and enriched as the relationship develops. 

A lead can include:  

- **Contact and company information:** Names, contact details, position, and associated company. 
- **Marketing campaign:** The campaign responsible for generating the lead. 
- **Product or solution interest:** The offerings that attracted the prospect. 
- **Business characteristics:** Industry, company size, sales area, and other relevant attributes. 
- **Additional notes:** Details gathered during conversations or research. 

This information helps your team understand where a lead originated, what the potential customer might need, and which aspects deserve further attention.  

As qualification progresses, the prospect can also be identified against your existing Person and Company records in **@@name**. 
This helps teams check for duplicates, recognize existing customers interested in additional products or services, and connect preliminary information with the customer's established business data in your **@@name** instance.  

The result is a practical distinction between **collecting initial interest** and **maintaining verified business relationships**, without mixing both stages into the same place. 

## Qualify Leads and Guide the Next Action

Name and email addresses alone do not establish viable sales opportunities.  

That's why qualification helps businesses learn more about the prospects, determine which of them have genuine commercial potential, and find the appropriate next actions to develop this potential.  

**@@name** supports this through a structured progression of lead stages.  


| Lead stage | Business purpose |
|---|---|
| **New** | Capture incoming interest and ensure every prospect receives initial attention and assessment. |
| **Qualifying** | Understand the prospect's needs, evaluate their potential, and determine whether further engagement is worthwhile. |
| **Marketing Qualified Lead (MQL)** | Identify promising prospects whose interest and characteristics justify more focused engagement and potential sales handoff. |
| **Sales Qualified Lead (SQL)** | Recognize prospects ready for direct sales engagement and potential conversion into opportunities (deals). |
| **Closed** | Conclude lead processing with a clear outcome, preserving its history without keeping completed leads in active qualification. |

Beyond these standard stages, **@@name** supports **user-defined lead stages**, allowing businesses to incorporate their established marketing and sales practices directly into the lead qualification process.  

These custom stages do more than indicate progress. 
They work together in pairs with the system lead stages to represent specific activities or steps, clarify where each prospect currently stands, and guide your team toward the **next appropriate action** in the qualification process.  

For example, stages such as *Awaiting Initial Contact*, *Clarifying Requirements*, or *Preparing Sales Discussion* can help your team understand what needs to happen next. 
Stage descriptions can provide additional guidance to your employees, helping them understand what is expected at each point of the process and apply a more consistent approach to engaging prospects.  

**@@name** also records when leads first reach the MQL and SQL stages, providing useful timestamps for evaluating key events against the timeline of the qualification progress.  

With all this, **@@name** enables you to embed your **real-world lead management processes and best practices into your @@name platform**. 
This helps your business create a consistent, actionable workflow that reflects how your team truly operates, while retaining the necessary flexibility to adapt it to your everyday operations.  

## Assign Responsibility and Coordinate Sales Engagement

**@@name** distinguishes between the user responsible for handling a lead record administratively and the salesperson assigned to engage with the potential customer.  

Salespeople can be assigned directly or through **rule-based assignments**.  

For example, when an identified company already has a customer record in your **@@name platform**, configured rules can automatically determine the appropriate salesperson based on existing customer-handling responsibilities.  

The system is also able to notify salespersons when they receive new assignments, helping responsible employees become aware of relevant changes.  

These capabilities support establishing clear accountability and organizing follow-ups around the people responsible for developing each prospect as the qualification progresses.  

## Convert Qualified Leads into Sales Opportunities

Lead qualification serves a practical purpose: helping you sieve which prospects are sufficiently promising to become actual sales opportunities.  

Once a lead has been successfully processed, **@@name** lets you convert it into an **Opportunity** (also known as a *Deal*).  

The original lead can retain a reference to the resulting opportunity, connecting the initial prospect with the subsequent commercial process.  

While **Lead Management** focuses on understanding and qualifying potential customers, **Opportunity Management** develops concrete potential deals.  

Opportunities include more concrete sales information, like expected revenue, probability of success, anticipated closing date, and the responsible salesperson for negotiations or further engagement.  

From there, **@@name's CRM system** takes your team through the next stages of the sales process, including preparing offers, processing sales orders, and managing invoicing.  

## What Lead Management Brings to Your Business

Lead Management provides distinct benefits across marketing and sales:

- **Marketing teams** can organize campaign-originating prospects, track qualification, and distinguish initial inquiries from more developed commercial interest. 
- **Sales representatives** gain relevant prospect information, clearly assigned responsibility, the contact means for the lead, and guidance on the next appropriate action. 
- **Sales managers** can follow through qualification progress, oversee responsibilities, and examine how incoming interest develops into sales opportunities. 

Together, these capabilities help your organization apply consistent lead-handling practices, focus sales efforts on promising prospects, and keep early-stage qualification separate from your clean sales activity.

Consider your company participating in an industry exhibition to introduce a new product range.  

During the event, your representatives meet potential customers with varying levels of interest. 
They immediately register these contacts as leads in **@@name** for your marketing campaign, recording their contact details, company information, and product interests.

Rather than treating every exhibition contact as an immediate deal, the company follows an established process for revealing true opportunities and directing sales effort where it matters.
